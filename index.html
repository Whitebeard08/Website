Why A2 · Why VedKumbh

Not all ghee is the same.
Ours starts with the right milk.

Most commercial ghee is made from mixed or A1 milk, processed fast at high heat. VedKumbh uses only the A2 milk of indigenous desi cows — and treats it the slow, traditional way so the flavour and character survive into the jar.

A2 milk, from desi cows

Indigenous Indian breeds naturally produce milk with only the A2 beta-casein protein — the milk Indian kitchens trusted for generations. We source from farmers we know, not anonymous tankers.

Curd-set Bilona method

Milk is boiled, set into curd, then churned slowly (bilona) to draw out white butter — which is simmered on a low flame into ghee. It takes ~25 litres of milk for a kilo of ghee. That's why it tastes like nothing else.

Tested, then packed in glass

Every batch is checked for purity and aroma before it ever meets a jar. We pack in food-safe glass — never thin plastic — so your ghee arrives as it left our kitchen: golden, grainy, fragrant.

From udder to jar

Four slow steps. Zero shortcuts.

  1. 01

    Select A2 milk

    Fresh morning & evening milk from desi cows, collected from trusted local dairy farmers around Kota.

  2. 02

    Set the curd

    Milk is gently boiled, cooled, and cultured overnight into dahi — the essential first step real Bilona demands.

  3. 03

    Churn the Bilona

    Curd is churned slowly in both directions to separate makhan (white butter), which is washed clean in water.

  4. 04

    Simmer & pack

    Butter is simmered on a low flame till golden, filtered, batch-checked, and sealed in glass jars for your kitchen.

Good to know

Questions, answered.

What makes A2 ghee different?

It starts with the milk. Indigenous desi cow breeds produce milk containing only the A2 type of beta-casein protein. Combined with our slow Bilona process, the result is a ghee with a distinctive grain, aroma, and taste that fast-processed ghee can't match.

What is the Bilona method?

Milk → boiled → set into curd → slow-churned (bilona) into white butter → simmered on low flame into ghee. It's labour-intensive (roughly 25 litres of milk per kilo of ghee) but preserves the traditional flavour and texture.

What's the shelf life? How do I store it?

9–12 months in a cool, dry place away from sunlight. Always use a dry spoon, keep the lid tight, and never refrigerate — real ghee stays perfectly happy at room temperature.
